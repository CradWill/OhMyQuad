# Form Research

## General Form Tips

- Most important thing is to select a weight you are certain you can handle, proper form is impossible on a load you cannot physically manage
- Correct stance in relation to barbell/dumbbell
- Proper grasp of the barbell/dumbbell
- Feet between hip and shoulder-width apart
- Squat down behind the bar or between dumbbells
- When lifting a bar, position it close to shins and over balls of the feet
  - Grasp with a closed grip at shoulder width apart
- Position the arms outside the knees, with elbows extended
- When lifting weight off floor, ensure back is flat or only slightly arched, and that the trapezius is relaxed and slightly stretched with chest held up and out
- Ensure head is in line with the spine
- Body weight is balanced between the middle and balls of the feet, but heels are touching the floor
- The shoulders are slightly in front of bar
- Eyes are looking straight ahead, or slightly up

## Squatting Tips

- Arguably the most dangerous, and hardest lift to get correct
- Many variations

### Traditional Barbell Squat

- Shoulder-width stance
- Holding barbell on top of the scapulae and trapezius
- Hands are slightly wider than the shoulders, toes may point slightly out
- Keep torso erect through motion, sit back with the hips until the top of the thighs are parallel with the floor
- Once there, extend the legs at the hips and knees to return to start

### Front Squat

- Shoulder-width stance
- Elbows positioned as high in front of the body as possible so the barbell rests on the front of the shoulder
- Hands are slightly wider than shoulders
- Keep torso erect through motion, lower body at the knees and hips until the top of the thighs are parallel to the floor
- Keep elbows elevated as high as possible
- Extend legs at the hips and knees to return to start

### Overhead Squat

- Shoulder-width stance
- Barbell held overhead with a snatch grip
- Push head through so that the arms are behind the ears in a locked out position
- Sit back with the hips until the top of the thighs are aligned parallel with the floor
- Keep torso in a rigid position while maintaining good balance
  - In the event you catch yourself falling, it is important to first throw the bar in front of you to prevent it from falling down on you
- Extend legs at the hips and knees to return to start

## Common Mistakes

- Using back to lift, rather than driving weight with legs
- Not having a strong grip on bar
- Misplaced grip, unsymmetrical
- Not clearing workout space of any items, or even people
- Going too high on weight load
- Too much bend in the back
- Fully locking limbs on presses
  - Can break your limbs in the most extreme cases
